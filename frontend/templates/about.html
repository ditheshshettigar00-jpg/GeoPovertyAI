About This Project

The problem

Reliable poverty/deprivation statistics usually come from large household surveys that are expensive, slow, and conducted only once every several years. Between survey rounds, and in areas with sparse coverage, there is very little fine-grained visibility into how conditions are changing.

The approach

Satellite imagery is captured repeatedly and covers the entire country, including remote areas. Published research has shown that visual patterns in imagery, and auxiliary layers like nighttime-light emissions, correlate with area-level economic activity and living standards. This project combines those signals with existing survey-based ground truth using machine learning, to estimate deprivation for districts, with every prediction accompanied by an explanation of what drove it.

Pipeline

Ground truth (NFHS-5 2019–21 socioeconomic indicators across 564 districts) → 10 satellite & geospatial features (VIIRS nightlights, Sentinel-2 NDVI, GHSL built-up & population, SRTM elevation & slope, CHIRPS rainfall, MODIS land surface temperature, ESA WorldCover cropland, and Oxford/MAP 2015 travel time via Google Earth Engine) → spatial Leave-One-State-Out cross-validation (Ridge, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost) → TreeSHAP explanations → this dashboard. (Exploratory VGG-16 image embeddings were tested and excluded after lowering out-of-state R².)

Limitations

  • District-level (not village/household) resolution, due to public data privacy constraints.
  • Associative, not causal, relationships between features and outcomes.
  • Dependent on the quality and age of the underlying survey ground truth.
  • OpenStreetMap POI tagging density varies significantly across India.
  • This is a student research prototype, not a validated operational tool.
